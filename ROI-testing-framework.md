# Roadmap to Independence - Testing Framework 

## Purpose

The roadmap defines each stage by an exit condition, not a declaration. This document exists to answer a single question honestly, on a recurring basis: *has the exit condition for our current stage actually been met, or have we only claimed it has?* It is designed for use by one movement at a time, but nothing in it is specific to any single movement — the indicators are built directly from the roadmap's own stage definitions, not from any particular context.

This is a measurement and feedback tool, not a management tool. It does not tell a movement what to do. It tells them, as accurately as their own honesty allows, where they actually are.

## Design principles

**Measure the exit condition, not activity.** It is easy to count meetings held, documents shared, or people who "support" something in the abstract. None of those numbers answer whether a stage's actual exit condition has been met. Every indicator below is written to test the exit condition directly, even when that is harder to measure than activity would be.

**Self-report, but structure the self-report to resist wishful thinking.** A movement assessing its own progress has an obvious incentive to see more progress than exists. The instruments below ask for specific, falsifiable evidence — a named disagreement that was actually resolved, a delegate mandate that was actually revoked — rather than a general impression, precisely to make optimistic rounding harder.

**Passive participation is data, not a deficiency.** Consistent with the roadmap itself, this framework tracks the passive-to-active spectrum without treating passive positions as failures to be corrected. A stage with a large, stable, informed passive base and a small tested active core is healthy. A stage with only active participants and no passive base is fragile and should be flagged as such.

**Protect the people being measured.** Any record of who is at which stage, who holds which role, or who attended what is itself sensitive information — precisely the kind of information that matters most under conditions of repression, which is when honest measurement matters most and is riskiest to collect. See the Data Protection section before collecting anything.

## How to use this framework

Run a self-assessment at a regular interval — quarterly is a reasonable default for a small movement, annually at minimum. Each assessment produces two things: a scorecard (below) and a short written note on anything that changed since the last assessment, especially anything that regressed. Regression is expected and should be recorded plainly, not treated as a reporting failure — the roadmap already establishes that movement backward through the stages is normal, not a sign the framework or the movement has failed.

## Stage indicators

For each stage, the framework gives: what to actually ask or measure, and what a genuine "yes" requires as evidence — not just a feeling that it's roughly true.

### Stage 3 — Voice within the system
- **Indicator:** Has a specific channel (named body, named process) been used, and did it respond?
- **Evidence required:** The specific request made, the specific response received, and whether the response changed anything material. "No response" and "acknowledged but nothing changed" are both valid, useful answers — record them as such rather than as an embarrassment.

### Stage 4 — Networked recognition
- **Indicator:** Do people who privately doubted the old order now know that others share that doubt?
- **Evidence required:** A rough, honestly-estimated count of people who have engaged with shared material (read, discussed, responded) — not a count of people merely present in a wider population who might agree if asked. The test is common knowledge, not aggregate sentiment: can members name other specific people or groups who they know share the doubt, not just guess that some proportion of the public probably does.
- **Watch for:** Any move toward drafting permanent structure at this stage is itself a negative indicator — record it as a flag, not a milestone.

### Stage 5 — Organized non-compliance (local)
- **Indicator:** Has the local unit's own decision-making or recall mechanism actually been exercised, under real disagreement, at least once?
- **Evidence required:** A specific disagreement, who was on each side, how it was resolved, and whether the losing side accepted the outcome and stayed. A local council that has never had a contested vote or a genuine internal dispute has not met this exit condition, regardless of how long it has existed.

### Stage 5.5 — Federated coordination (the moot)
- **Indicator:** Has the federation-level body survived a real disagreement between member units without either leaving, and has a delegate mandate actually been renewed or revoked?
- **Evidence required:** The specific disagreement between units and its resolution; the specific delegate whose mandate was renewed or revoked, and by what process. A moot that has only ever discussed points of agreement has not been tested.
- **Watch for:** Any function moving from "coordinated at the moot" to "decided at the moot and simply reported to local units" between one assessment and the next — this is the earliest measurable sign of quiet re-centralization, and should be flagged the moment it's observed rather than after it has become established practice.

### Stage 6b — Obsolescence (the primary target measure)
- **Indicator:** What share of the concrete functions people actually rely on day to day are now handled by federated structures rather than the old system?
- **Evidence required:** A short, fixed list of specific functions (decided once per movement, kept stable across assessments so trends are comparable — for example: dispute resolution, resource distribution, information and communication, physical security, provisioning) with a simple three-way rating per function: still old-system, mixed/transitional, or federated. Track the list over time rather than producing a single abstract "percentage independent" figure, which invites rounding up.
- **Watch for:** A function rated "federated" where people cannot in practice recall or exit the structure providing it — this fails the legitimacy test even if the function itself has moved, and should be rated as mixed/transitional, not federated, until it passes both parts of the test.

### Stage 7 — Institutionalized reversibility
- **Indicator:** Has any recall or exit mechanism actually been invoked, anywhere in the structure, since the last assessment?
- **Evidence required:** If yes — what was invoked, by whom, and what happened. If no — an honest note on whether that's because nothing needed it, or because no one has tried. A long silence on this indicator is itself informative and should not be read as good news by default.

## The passive–active spectrum measure

For each active stage (4 through 5.5), record two numbers where feasible: a rough count of people engaged at all (passive end included), and a count of people who hold a specific, nameable, active role (organizer, delegate, documented contributor). Track the ratio over time rather than either number alone. A shrinking active core with a stable or growing passive base is a different situation from both numbers shrinking together, and the response to each should differ — the framework's job is only to surface which situation is occurring, not to prescribe the response.

## Data protection

Everything this framework asks a movement to record — who holds which role, who was on which side of a disagreement, who attended a moot — is exactly the information that matters most to an adversary under conditions of surveillance or repression. Before collecting anything:

- Decide, in advance, who has access to assessment records and where they are stored.
- Default to aggregate and anonymized figures (counts, ratios, function-status ratings) in anything that leaves the smallest trusted group. Named specifics (who, exactly, resolved which disagreement) can stay within the local unit that generated them rather than being compiled centrally.
- Treat the assessment records themselves as a stage 7 concern — they are only useful if the people who generated them can trust how they're held, which is the same trust the whole roadmap depends on.

## Using this beyond one movement

Nothing here refers to any specific movement's context, deliberately. A second movement using this framework should replace the function list under stage 6b with their own, keep their own function list stable across their own assessments, and otherwise use the indicators as written. Comparing results *between* movements is not the framework's purpose and should be done cautiously if at all — the point of each assessment is a movement's honesty with itself over time, not a ranking against anyone else's.
